*Brian | HKEX · 0388.HK | Research as of November 11, 2026 | [Full research report (Word)](https://brian936826-lang.github.io/posts/hkex-market-of-markets/HKEX-The-Market-of-Markets.docx)*

On May 22, 2026, China's securities regulator issued advance notices of administrative penalties to entities associated with Tiger, Futu and Longbridge for unlawful cross-border business. The enforcement plan announced that day extended its scope to existing business, trading instructions and fund transfers, while explicitly directing Mainland investors toward authorized channels such as Southbound Stock Connect and QDII for overseas investment.[^brokers]

The plan addressed unlawful cross-border services while identifying the investment channels that remained available. Investors want to allocate assets overseas, and the rules determine how they can trade. Who operates the connections they use?

That is what draws me to HKEX. Investors choose securities, brokers serve clients, and exchanges and clearing houses handle the resulting trades. Different institutions build different businesses around the same assets. I favor the infrastructure that can serve the whole market over the long term, and I want to understand how that position translates into shareholder returns.

## From authorized access to a working business

A Mainland investor buying Hong Kong-listed securities through Stock Connect uses the Southbound route; an international investor buying eligible A-shares through Hong Kong uses the Northbound route. HKEX helps connect the trading and clearing arrangements on both sides. An IPO in Hong Kong brings a company's financing needs to the market: the proceeds go to the company, and subsequent trading in its shares continues to use the same market infrastructure.[^connect]

{{< hkex-exhibit kind="routes" >}}

The explicit preservation of authorized channels gives these connections a basis for continued business. Their value to HKEX has two parts: existing trading continues to use its services, while new participants and more active trading expand revenue. The first concerns how much business it can retain; the second determines how much it can grow.

When an order that was already being executed in Hong Kong moves to a different broker or access route, the first change is in the client relationship. The effect on HKEX's revenue depends on turnover, fees and revenue sharing. That is why my long-term case also rests on Chinese assets attracting more participation.

Chinese companies' competitiveness in advanced manufacturing, artificial intelligence and global supply chains is creating new investment opportunities. Investors study earnings and prices, allocate capital, then adjust their portfolios and manage risk. I expect the range of activity around Chinese assets to keep expanding. HKEX serves trading across many companies and industries, which is why I want to participate in this trend through it.

Hong Kong has an established market network and faces competition from other channels. Qualified foreign institutions can enter Mainland markets through QFI, while international investors can also choose overseas-listed securities or funds.[^access] HKEX needs to retain demand within its network through ease of access, market depth and efficient services.

This is already a substantial business. In the first nine months of 2026, Stock Connect generated HK$4.289 billion in revenue and other income for HKEX, approximately 17% of the group total. Trading on the two routes diverged: Northbound average daily turnover rose 61% year over year, while Southbound turnover fell 7%.[^connect] Northbound trading expanded faster during the period. The effects of the enforcement measures affecting Mainland investors discussed at the start will need to be assessed through subsequent changes in Southbound clients and trading.

## How a trade becomes revenue

A client places an order with a broker, the order is executed in the relevant market, and clearing and settlement complete the exchange of money and securities. HKEX charges trading and clearing participants for the services it provides. Brokerage commissions, taxes and capital raised by companies each go to their respective recipients.[^fees]

I am more interested in the years of trading after a company lists. Each time a share changes hands, trading and clearing services are used. Later, investors adjust positions, market makers manage inventory, and institutions hedge risk, generating further trades. These recurring activities explain why trading and clearing account for the largest share of revenue.

Holding and trading securities create different service needs. Long-term holdings require services such as custody; frequent trading generates trading and clearing revenue more directly. To assess growth in this business, I look first at turnover, then at how much fee revenue that turnover actually produces.

The original report also discusses opportunities from tokenized equities and new derivatives. New products attract traders, and market makers need to manage risk. Some of that demand may be met by trading the underlying securities. If those hedges are executed through Stock Connect, they generate activity on which HKEX earns fees. I am positive on the potential for new products to drive underlying trading; the scale will depend on the hedging methods and trading routes market makers use.

## As the business grows

The strength of this business builds through specific connections. Operating an exchange or clearing house requires regulatory recognition. As buyers and sellers gather, market depth and execution efficiency attract more participants. Financial institutions also connect their clearing, settlement, collateral and risk management to the same arrangements. Changing providers requires those relationships to be reworked.[^infrastructure]

Brokers compete for clients through commissions and service. Within Stock Connect, the resulting orders use designated routing and cross-border clearing arrangements. HKEX can therefore continue to serve the business brought in by different brokers. I value its enduring role in the infrastructure the market shares.

The financial attraction comes from repeated use of the systems. Existing infrastructure can handle more trading, allowing revenue to grow faster than staff and technology costs. In 2025, for example, growth in group revenue and other income substantially outpaced growth in operating expenses, amplifying profit growth.[^financials]

{{< hkex-exhibit kind="leverage" >}}

The same cost structure helps explain the slowdown in the third quarter of 2026. Revenue increased only slightly, while spending on staff, technology and regulatory requirements continued. Expenses grew faster than revenue, and profit attributable to shareholders fell.[^financials] HKEX's long-term business strengths and its short-term earnings cycle appear in the same financial statements.

Investment income also contributes to profit. The group's own funds, participants' margin funds and clearing house funds earn returns, with income depending on the amount invested and the rate of return.[^investment] For shareholders, trading revenue growth, continued spending and changes in investment income must ultimately translate into cash available for distribution. In 2025, HKEX paid HK$13.722 billion in cash dividends to its shareholders, a return its fee-generating business has already delivered.[^cash]

The immediate question is how cooler trading will affect the next phase of earnings. In October 2026, Hong Kong market average daily turnover was approximately HK$180.65 billion, compared with HK$272.9 billion in the first nine months. Northbound turnover declined from an average of RMB332.4 billion to approximately RMB250.3 billion over the same comparison periods.[^turnover] How long these trading levels persist will affect how much of current earnings can be sustained, and what price to pay for the business today.

## Connecting the business to the purchase price

I am bullish on HKEX's long-term position and pay close attention to the price I would pay for it. The recent slowdown in trading puts earnings durability at the center of the immediate debate. How far participation in Chinese assets can expand determines growth over the longer term. I consider both time horizons when assessing the price.

The original report uses a reference share price of HK$383.20 on November 11, 2026. Approximate trailing twelve-month EPS, calculated from disclosed earnings, was HK$15.63, implying a P/E of 24.52×.[^valuation] For context, I use 22 annual observations following results releases from 2005 through 2026. Their median P/E is 33.43× and their lower quartile is 29.66×. These annual snapshots use the previous year's basic EPS as newly disclosed at each observation date; the current multiple uses rolling TTM EPS.[^sample]

{{< hkex-exhibit kind="valuation" >}}

The report's 30× scenario is close to the sample's lower quartile and approximately 10.3% below its median. Holding EPS at HK$15.63 implies a share price of HK$468.90, approximately 22.4% above the reference price. This price upside comes from the market assigning a higher value to the same earnings. Earnings growth driven by trading activity is a separate source of returns.

My medium- to long-term BUY view on HKEX is unchanged. Demand to participate in Chinese assets, an established fee-generating business, and a valuation below the historical reference points at the research date together make it attractive. Authorized connections provide the operating foundation; participants' actual trades turn that infrastructure into a business; greater scale then converts revenue into profit. That is what I mean by “The Market of Markets.”

I will continue to watch Hong Kong and Northbound turnover, along with the profit left from trading and clearing revenue after ongoing spending. Persistently weaker trading or a declining share of fees would lead me to lower my assessment of the business. I expect broader participation in Chinese assets to become increasingly visible in HKEX's revenue, profit and cash returns to shareholders.
