The Market of Markets: Why I Am Bullish on HKEX

As cross-border investment routes narrow, I am drawn to the infrastructure that keeps markets connected. This is my investment case for HKEX.
November 12, 2026 · 9 min · Wentao Xin (Brian) | Translations:
  • 中文

In May, China’s securities regulator announced proposed penalties against entities associated with Tiger, Futu and Longbridge for unlawful cross-border business. The accompanying enforcement campaign covered existing unlicensed business, order handling and fund transfers, while directing investors toward authorized channels such as Southbound Stock Connect and QDII. 1

In July, new offshore trust tax rules took effect. Chinese tax-resident individuals who contribute assets to offshore trusts must report the trusts’ income even when it has not been distributed. The rules also address previously unpaid tax. 2

In September, an entity holding Haidilao shares through Shu Ping’s family trust sold 259 million shares. Caixin reported market speculation linking the sale to offshore trust taxation. 3

These developments raised a question for me: Demand for cross-border investment remains, while the available routes are narrowing. Who operates the gateways that stay open?

My answer is HKEX. I am bullish on its ability to earn fees by connecting Mainland China with international capital markets. As more investors participate in Chinese assets through Hong Kong, buying, selling, rebalancing and hedging repeatedly use this infrastructure. That is the core of my medium- to long-term BUY view.

I call this business The Market of Markets.

Chinese assets create an opportunity for market infrastructure#

Chinese companies’ competitiveness in advanced manufacturing, artificial intelligence and global supply chains is creating new investable assets. International interest in these businesses brings demand for portfolio allocation, rebalancing, market making and risk management. HKEX can serve that activity across many companies and industries.

I expect Chinese assets to be traded and priced by a broader investor base. International investors need access to Mainland markets; Mainland investors also want exposure to Hong Kong-listed securities. Hong Kong’s financial institutions and Stock Connect already provide established routes for both.

Regulatory separation increases the value of authorized connections. Mainland and international markets differ in access rules, account structures and clearing arrangements. Infrastructure that bridges those differences has a service worth charging for.

I also see potential demand from tokenized equities and new derivatives. Market makers managing their exposures may need to trade the underlying securities. When those hedges are executed through Stock Connect, they generate transactions on which HKEX earns fees. New investment platforms can expand the market served by existing infrastructure.

How does that demand reach Hong Kong?#

International investors buying A-shares, Mainland investors buying Hong Kong securities, and companies listing in Hong Kong follow three distinct routes. The first two generate recurring trading activity. The third brings corporate financing to Hong Kong and adds securities that investors can subsequently trade.

In the first nine months of 2026, Stock Connect generated HK$4.289 billion in revenue and other income for HKEX, approximately 17% of the group total. Connecting markets is already a substantial business.

Hong Kong’s advantage combines market access, liquidity and institutional networks. QFI, overseas listings and funds also provide exposure to Chinese assets. HKEX needs to keep attracting participants through market depth and efficient services.

The value is in recurring fees#

I am especially interested in the years of trading that follow a company’s listing. Investors buying, selling and rebalancing use execution, clearing and settlement services. Those services can earn fees regardless of whether an individual investment makes money.

In the first nine months of 2026, trading and clearing-related fees accounted for 62.5% of group revenue and other income. Listing fees contributed 6.3%. Recurring trading activity is the center of this business.

The group’s services also span derivatives, the London Metal Exchange, custody and market data. Stock Connect brings cross-border demand for Chinese assets into a wider infrastructure business with multiple sources of revenue.

How does this position translate into profit?#

HKEX has three mutually reinforcing barriers to entry: regulatory authorization, concentrated liquidity and its clearing network.

Operating an exchange or clearing house requires regulatory recognition. Bringing buyers and sellers together improves execution and reduces market impact, attracting more investors and issuers. Once institutions connect, their accounts, collateral, settlement and risk management processes operate around that network. Switching costs further strengthen its position.

More participants deepen liquidity, and deeper liquidity attracts more participants. Brokers compete for clients through commissions and service, while Stock Connect orders continue to use designated routing and clearing infrastructure.

I prefer owning the shared infrastructure that serves the trading activity these brokers bring to the market.

The financial benefit is that additional trading can use systems already in place. In 2025, group revenue grew 30.3%, operating expenses rose only 5.3%, and profit attributable to shareholders increased 36.0%. Revenue growth outpaced cost growth, amplifying profit.

HKEX also paid HK$13.722 billion in dividends to shareholders that year. Its ability to earn fees is already delivering cash to investors.

The third quarter of 2026 showed operating leverage working in the other direction: revenue rose approximately 1% year over year, expenses grew 8%, and profit attributable to shareholders fell approximately 1%. Staff, technology and regulatory spending continue when trading slows, putting pressure on profit.

In October, Hong Kong market average daily turnover was approximately HK$180.65 billion, below the first nine months’ average of HK$272.9 billion. Northbound average daily turnover was approximately RMB250.3 billion, below the earlier average of RMB332.4 billion.

The investment debate is whether this is a temporary trading slowdown or the start of a lower earnings base. My long-term view remains positive; subsequent turnover and profit will test that view.

What price am I willing to pay?#

At the research date, the reference share price was HK$383.20. Approximate trailing twelve-month EPS, calculated from reported earnings, was HK$15.63, implying a 24.52× P/E.

My sample of 22 annual observations following earnings releases from 2005 through 2026 has a median P/E of 33.43× and a lower quartile of 29.66×. The research-date multiple sits below these historical reference points. Combined with the business’s long-term barriers to entry, I find the price attractive.

With TTM EPS held at HK$15.63, a 30× P/E implies HK$468.90, 22.4% above the reference price. That multiple is near the sample’s lower quartile and 10.3% below its median. My case depends on earnings holding up and a partial valuation recovery.

The combination I like is broader participation in Chinese assets, HKEX retaining its ability to earn fees, and an entry multiple below its historical reference points. Business growth and market repricing can both contribute to shareholder returns.

What would change my view?#

What I watchSupports the BUY caseWeakens the BUY case
Trading demandHong Kong and Northbound turnover recover; new products and participants generate tradingTrading stays weak or shifts to other venues
Earnings deliveryTrading and clearing revenue grows faster than operating costsLower fees or revenue shares, rising costs and weaker investment income erode profit
Institutional positionStock Connect expands and existing networks serve more demandAccess restrictions suppress participation, or changes to trading and clearing arrangements weaken HKEX’s fee position

If participation in Chinese assets continues to translate into HKEX revenue, its infrastructure advantages can compound into shareholder returns. Sustained weak trading or an erosion of its fee position would lead me to lower my assessment.

As of November 11, 2026, I maintain a medium- to long-term BUY view on HKEX. I want to own infrastructure serving an expanding market for Chinese assets. Companies, investors and products will change; HKEX continues to connect markets and earn revenue from their use.
